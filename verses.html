Multiverses of Various Dimensions

This page contains a list of names for n-D polyverses, such as polyhedron for 3-D. The names polygon and polyhedron were known for a long time. Multichoron was coined by Norman Johnson by shortening George Olshevsky's polychorema. Later George and Jonathan coined names such as polytetron, polypenton, and polyhexon - however these names had a problem - for example is a hecatonicosatetron a 120 sided polytetron (5-D) or a 100 sided icosatetron (25-D) - because of this Wendy Kreiger came up with a way to avoid this confusion by calling the 5-D polytope a polyteron - she coined the names for 5-D up to 9-D by using the prefixes used in the metric system - tera, peta, exa, zetta, yotto, ronna, and quetta - these prefixes are actually a shortened or modified form of the Greek prefixes tetra, penta, hexa, hepta, octa, nona, and deca. Until October 5th 2025, Jonathan Bowers later extended this process to go up to a tredecillion multiverses - his name for a 25-D polytope is a polyicteron - where ic is short for icosi and tera is short for tetra. Also notice that the suffixes are for one less than the multiverse - ie 5-D is polyteron where tera is short for tetra = 4 - this is because a polyteron has many terons (4-D elements), not many petons (5-D elements) - however a polyteron can be a peton of a polypeton (6-D). To get the plural of the polytope (for multiverses 3 and up), change on to a - for example the plural of polyictron is polyictra, for multiverses 2 and down just add an s. The plurals to polytope elements are found by adding an s - i.e. the plurals for teron and ictron are terons and ictrons.
